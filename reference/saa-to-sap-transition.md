# Transition from SAA to SAP

Passing SAA proves associate-level architecture knowledge. It is the start of the professional path, not evidence that SAP should be booked immediately.

## Change the type of practice

Associate practice asks, "Which service or pattern best satisfies these requirements?"

Professional practice adds:

- Conflicting business and technical constraints
- Existing systems that cannot be replaced at once
- Multiple accounts, teams, and ownership boundaries
- Hybrid connectivity and identity
- Quotas, migrations, contracts, and operational readiness
- Partial failures and rollback
- Total portfolio cost rather than one workload
- Several technically valid options with different organizational consequences

## Build an experience portfolio

For each advanced capstone, retain redacted evidence:

- Requirements and assumptions
- Architecture decision records
- Threat model and trust boundaries
- Failure-mode analysis
- RTO/RPO mapping and recovery evidence
- Cost model and allocation
- Deployment, migration, and rollback plan
- Observability and incident runbook
- Risks accepted and owner
- Lessons from a failed assumption

Use synthetic workloads and non-sensitive identifiers.

## Professional-level gate

You are approaching level 400 when you can:

1. Find missing requirements before selecting services.
2. Identify account, Region, network, identity, data, and team boundaries.
3. Explain how the design changes during migration, failure, and recovery.
4. Quantify major scale, availability, and cost assumptions.
5. Reject a simpler-looking answer because it violates one hard requirement.
6. Reject an overengineered answer because its complexity is unnecessary.
7. Design the operating model, not only the deployment diagram.
8. Defend residual risk to a review board.

AWS describes the SAP target candidate as having two or more years of experience designing and implementing AWS solutions. Use this course to structure that experience, not to relabel study time as production experience.
